Kaldanit, a monospaced Hebrew typeface built on the Courier Prime cell, in Regular, Bold and Italic, designed by Nir Zabari Yenni for Nomad Type

Kaldanit is a monospaced Hebrew typeface. It is built on the cell of Courier Prime, the typewriter face by Alan Dague-Greene for Quote-Unquote Apps: the same 2048 units per em, the same 1228 unit advance for every character, the same cap height, x-height and vertical metrics. A Hebrew line and a Latin line of the same length therefore occupy exactly the same width, which is what a monospace is for. Screenplays, terminals, code, subtitles and tables keep their grid when the script changes.

The drawing does not copy Courier Prime, it answers it. Hebrew has no ascender and descender rhythm to lean on, so the weight sits in the horizontals, the joins stay open, and the letters are widened to fill a cell that was cut for a narrower alphabet. The references are Hebrew typewriters and an old FrankRuehl variant rather than a Latin slab.

Still to come: a Bold Italic, and mark positioning for the vowel points. The three upright and italic styles are finished, and they are what this page shows. The seventeen nikud marks are drawn and encoded, but each one still sits in its own cell, the way a typewriter's dead keys did, rather than attaching to the letter.
